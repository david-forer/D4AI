## AI consultant vs agency for a small business: the short answer

For a small business weighing an AI consultant against an agency, how much of your operation is written down decides it. Budget sets the ceiling. Inside that ceiling, the right provider is the one whose working method matches how documented your processes already are.

An agency builds against a specification. A fractional technology leader directs a function that exists. A systems integrator connects systems that are already stable. Each of those 3 assumes documentation that most firms at this size do not have yet.

A solo consultant is the only one of the 4 whose first deliverable is the documentation itself.

That single distinction resolves the choice faster than any comparison of headcount, portfolio, or price. If you can hand a provider a written description of how a job moves from enquiry to invoice, all 4 options are open. If you cannot, one of them is.

## What a small business is choosing between when it compares a consultant and an agency

The question is usually framed as 2 options. There are 4, and the middle 2 get skipped because they have unfamiliar names.

**The solo AI consultant.** One person, often with an operations background, who runs discovery, produces findings, and either builds directly or specifies the build for someone else. Engagements are scoped and finite. What that role involves day to day is covered in [what an AI consultant actually does](/blog/what-does-an-ai-consultant-do).

**The AI or automation agency.** A team with a sales function, a delivery function, and usually a fixed methodology. Strongest when the work is well defined and needs several people building in parallel.

**The fractional CTO or fractional operations leader.** A part-time executive embedded for a set number of hours a week, typically on a monthly retainer, directing technology or operations rather than doing the build.

**The systems integrator.** A firm that connects existing software so data moves between tools. Their work begins where your systems and their data structures are stable.

Most founders at $1M to $5M in revenue with no operations lead compare option 1 and option 2 only, because those are the 2 that advertise to them.

## Why documentation decides this more than budget

A business with undocumented processes has its operating knowledge in 3 or 4 people's heads. The steps vary by who runs them. The exceptions are invisible until someone hits one.

Hand that business to an agency and the first thing they ask for is a specification. Someone has to write it. That someone is the founder, at night, describing a process they have never had to make explicit. The spec comes back partial, the build matches the spec, and the gap surfaces in month 3 as a change request.

Hand it to a fractional CTO and the first 90 days go into discovery and documentation, at a monthly retainer priced for strategic direction.

Hand it to a systems integrator and the connections work until the underlying process changes, which at a growing firm happens quarterly.

A solo consultant working the same engagement starts with the process map because there is nothing else to start with. The documentation is the first deliverable rather than a prerequisite, which is why the sequence fits a business that has never written its operations down.

Agencies and fractional leaders become the right answer once the documentation exists. Several of the firms that use them well hired a consultant first to produce it.

## Where each option goes wrong at this size

**Agencies.** The failure is a competent build aimed at a step that was not the constraint. It looks like success at handover and stalls in adoption. This pattern is common enough that it has its own post on [automation that fails because the audit was skipped](/blog/automation-failed-skipped-audit).

**Fractional CTOs.** The failure is scale mismatch. A fractional CTO priced at $10,000 a month is built to lead a technology function with engineers in it. At a 14-person service firm there is usually no function to lead, and the retainer buys strategy for a business whose constraint is a scheduling process nobody has mapped.

**Systems integrators.** The failure is brittleness. The integration is correct on the day it ships and breaks when a form field changes or the team adopts a new tool.

**Solo consultants.** The failure is capacity. One person can only build so much, and a project needing 3 parallel workstreams will run longer with one consultant than with a team. The honest answer when a scope is too big for one person is a consultant who specifies the work and an agency that builds it.

## The fractional ops leader question, specifically

The comparison founders find hardest is a consultant against a fractional operations leader, because both are one person and both talk about systems.

The difference is duration and output. A consultant is hired to close a defined gap and leave, and the deliverable is a working system plus the documentation for it. A fractional operations leader is hired to hold a role, and the deliverable is decisions made well, week after week.

That makes the question about recurrence. A business that needs 1 decision made properly, about how intake or scheduling or reporting should work, is buying a project. A business making that class of decision every week, and currently routing all of it through the founder, is short a role.

At 5 to 20 staff it is usually a project. The recurring decisions that justify a permanent operations seat tend to appear once a firm passes 25 people or runs more than 1 delivery model at the same time.

## A 5-question readiness check

Answer these before any sales call. Count the yeses.

1. Could a new hire run your core delivery process from a written document, without asking anyone?
2. Do you know, in hours, how long each step of that process takes?
3. Is there one named person other than you who owns how work moves through the business?
4. Are your client records in one system that everyone uses?
5. Could you write down what a successful project would change, in a number, this afternoon?

4 or 5 yeses means an agency, a fractional leader, or an integrator can do useful work immediately, and you should choose on scope and price.

2 or 3 yeses means start with a consultant-led scoping engagement, then decide who builds.

0 or 1 yeses means the documentation work is the project. Anyone you hire will do it first, and a solo consultant is the cheapest way to have it done properly. If you are unsure whether the timing is right at all, [are you ready to hire an AI consultant](/blog/are-you-ready-to-hire-an-ai-consultant) covers the prerequisites.

## When more than one option is right

Sequencing 2 providers is common and usually cheaper than picking one and hoping.

A scoping engagement with a consultant produces the process map, the priority, and a specification. That specification is what lets you run a competitive quote with 2 or 3 agencies, because they are pricing the same defined thing rather than guessing at your business.

Founders who do this report a narrower spread between quotes, which is the practical sign that the spec is doing its job.

The reverse order, hiring an agency first and asking them to define the scope, puts the specification in the hands of the party that will be paid to build it. Plenty of agencies handle that responsibly. The incentive still sits in one place.

## How to run this decision this week

Score the 5 questions without rounding up, write the number down, and let it pick the provider type before you take any calls. Then compare candidates inside that type only.

Shortlisting across types is what makes this decision feel impossible, because a $6,000 scoping engagement and a $60,000 build are not comparable proposals. They answer different questions about a business at different stages.

Once the type is set, the evaluation is a normal one, and the framework in [how to choose an AI consultant](/blog/how-to-choose-an-ai-consultant) applies directly. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should a small business hire an AI consultant or an AI agency?

It depends on whether your processes are written down. An agency builds to a specification, so without one the founder ends up writing it at night. If you can't describe on paper how a job moves from enquiry to invoice, start with a consultant.

### Do I need a fractional CTO or an AI consultant for a 10-person company?

Usually a consultant. A fractional CTO is built to lead a technology function with engineers in it, and most 10-person firms do not have one to lead. Pay for the problem you have, not the org chart you might have someday.

### When does a small business need a systems integrator instead of an AI consultant?

When your systems are stable, your data is clean, and the job is getting data to move between tools. If your processes still change every quarter, the integration will break when they do.

### Can I use an AI consultant and an agency together?

Yes, and it is often cheaper. The consultant produces the process map and a specification, then 2 or 3 agencies quote against the same defined job. You end up with quotes you can actually compare.

### How much does an AI consultant cost compared to an agency?

Solo consultants run roughly $150 to $400 an hour or a fixed scope, while agency projects start around $50,000 with retainers from $8,000 a month. The bands are directional, so use them to rule options in or out rather than to judge a specific quote.
