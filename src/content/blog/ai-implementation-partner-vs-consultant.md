An AI consultant works out what should be built and in what order. An AI implementation partner builds it. For a small business the useful question is who writes the specification between those two jobs, because that document is where most AI projects go wrong.

This page covers what each one delivers, where each engagement ends and how to tell which one you need first. If you want the wider comparison with agencies and fractional leaders, start with [AI consultant vs agency](/blog/ai-consultant-vs-agency).

## What an AI Consultant Delivers

A consultant starts with your operation as it runs today. In my audits that means interviews with the people who do the work, a process map for each of the top jobs, a ranked list of opportunities with the hours and dollars attached, and a roadmap that names what to build first.

The output is a set of documents you own. A consultant's work is finished when you know what to build, why it comes first and what it should do. Some consultants stop there. Others, including me, also build.

The day-to-day work is covered in [what an AI consultant does](/blog/what-does-an-ai-consultant-do).

## What an AI Implementation Partner Delivers

A partner builds and connects the systems. That means setting up the automation in a tool like Make or n8n, wiring it to your CRM and inbox, testing it on real past cases, training your staff and supporting it after launch.

The output is a working system. The partner's work is finished when the thing runs and your team can use it. A good partner is fast and careful at building, and they depend on a clear description of what to build.

## Where Each Engagement Ends

| | Consultant | Implementation partner |
|---|---|---|
| Starts from | Your current operation | A written description of what to build |
| Main output | Process maps, findings, roadmap | A working system |
| Ends when | You know what to build and in what order | The system runs and your team uses it |
| Typical billing | Fixed fee per audit tier | Fixed project fee or monthly |

The table shows the handoff. The consultant's last page is the partner's first page.

## Who Writes the Specification

Here is the gap. A 14-person accounting practice decides it wants to automate client onboarding and hires a builder. The builder asks what happens when a client sends the engagement letter back unsigned. Nobody has an answer, because the senior bookkeeper handles it from memory. The builder makes a reasonable guess, builds it and bills for the changes when the guess turns out wrong.

That scenario is common because the specification is nobody's job. The partner assumes the client has one. The client assumes the partner will figure it out. A consultant who maps the process first writes that document, and a partner can then build from it without guessing.

## When to Hire an Implementation Partner First

Hire a partner first when three things are already true. You have your process written down in enough detail that a stranger could follow it. You have chosen the tools, or have a short list. And you know the job you want built. A legal practice manager who has already documented intake for a 6-attorney firm and wants it built inside Clio is a good fit.

If you are unsure about any of the three, the partner will either stop to ask questions you can't answer or guess.

## When to Hire a Consultant First

Hire a consultant first when you know the symptom and not the cause. Orders get stuck, the founder answers every question, or the team uses three tools that don't talk to each other. A consultant finds out which job costs the most and what fixing it is worth.

This is also the right order when you have a budget in mind and want to know how far it goes. Our [AI consulting cost guide](/blog/ai-consulting-cost-for-small-business) shows what each type of engagement runs. The audit produces the number that tells you whether a build is worth its price.

## Can One Person Do Both?

Yes, and for a business with 5 to 20 people it often works well. I run both stages. The audit comes first at $750, $1,500 or $4,000 depending on depth, and an install is quoted afterward at $7,500, $11,000 or $15,000 based on what the audit found. Scope is fixed in writing before either starts.

Having one person do both removes the handoff, so nothing gets lost between the findings and the build. The tradeoff is that you are relying on one person for both, so ask to see the audit deliverable before you commit to the build. You can take it to a different builder if you prefer, since the documents are yours.

## Four Questions to Ask Any Provider

- Who writes the specification, and do I get a copy?
- What do I own at the end, besides the finished system?
- What happens if you build something that doesn't match how my team really works?
- Can you show me a past project of my size?

## Questions Founders Ask About Implementation Partners

### What Is the Difference Between an AI Consultant and an AI Implementation Partner?

A consultant decides what to build and documents it. An implementation partner builds it from that specification. Some firms do both, and some consultants build.

### Do I Need Both?

Most small businesses need both stages in some form. The decision is the order. If your processes are documented, start with the partner. If they aren't, start with the consultant.

### Which Costs More?

A build usually costs more than an audit because it takes more hours of work. Ranges for each are in the [AI consulting cost guide](/blog/ai-consulting-cost-for-small-business).

### Can I Hire a Consultant and Use a Different Partner to Build?

Yes, if the consultant hands over the process maps and specification as documents you own. Ask about this before you sign.
